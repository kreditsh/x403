# x403: a risk decision before money moves

**Version 0.2, draft. October 2026.**
**Status:** implemented by kredit as the reference authority; open for other authorities, executors, requesters and evidence issuers.
**Versions:** the protocol version is the `version` field of every object and changes only when an object changes. The packages (`@kredit/x403` on npm, `x403` on PyPI) have their own release numbers; the README says which package releases speak which protocol version. See [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md).

## 0. TLDR

x401 says who is asking. x402 says how to pay. Neither says whether the payment should happen. x403 is the question in between: the party about to move money asks a risk authority, gets back a signed yes, not yet, or no, with reasons in a shared vocabulary and never a number, bound to that one payment and that one executor, and both sides keep a receipt anyone can verify later. After the money moves, the executor reports what happened, so the next decision is better than this one.

## 1. Definition

x403 is a request and response protocol by which the party about to execute an agentic payment or transfer, called the **executor** (a merchant, PSP, acquirer, vault, wallet or issuer processor), obtains a **signed risk decision** from a **risk authority** before executing, and both sides keep a **portable receipt**.

The decision is one state, `allowed`, `escalated` or `denied`, computed from five things:

1. the **payment** about to happen,
2. the **actor chain** behind it, human to operator to agent to instance,
3. the **intent** declared for it and the mandate it runs under,
4. the **evidence** presented, made by other protocols,
5. the actor's **history**, as the authority holds it.

The decision is explained with **reason codes** from a published registry, never with a number. It is bound to one payment (`subject`) and one executor (`audience`). After execution an **outcome report** closes the loop.

x403 sits between x401 (identity) and x402, MPP and the card rails (payment), and between mandate formats (AP2, Verifiable Intent, Amex Proof of Intent, Visa VIC instructions) and their execution. It consumes their artifacts as evidence and issues nothing that competes with them.

### 1.1 Terminology

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are to be read as in RFC 2119.

- **Executor:** the party that moves the money and asks before doing so: a merchant, PSP, acquirer, vault, wallet, card program or issuer processor.
- **Requester:** whoever signs and sends the risk query; the executor on the merchant leg, the operator's wallet or card program on the buyer leg.
- **Risk authority:** the party that computes and signs the decision.
- **Agent:** a registered software identity with a key, acting for an operator and a human principal.
- **Instance:** one session of an agent, holding a short-lived key or token.
- **Principal:** the human on whose behalf the payment is made.
- **Verifier:** anyone checking a decision or receipt offline: a dispute desk, an auditor, a regulator, the next executor.
- **Reporter:** a party that posts an outcome: the executor named in the receipt, the requester, the authority, or a party registered in the executor's policy.

## 2. Design principles

1. **A decision, not a number.** Reasons, never a score. Any numeric value stays inside the authority. A conformant decision contains no field whose value is a risk, trust or confidence number.
2. **Both legs, one object.** The buyer leg (an operator's wallet or card program asking before its agent pays) and the merchant leg (an executor asking before accepting) use the same query and the same decision. `direction` is a field.
3. **Consume, don't compete.** x401 results, AP2 mandates, Verifiable Intent chains, Amex Proof of Intent tokens, Visa VIC instructions, TAP and Mastercard WBA signatures, Skyfire KYA tokens, Stripe Shared Payment Tokens, MPP challenges, ERC-8004 registrations are **evidence**. x403 defines how they are referenced and hashed, not how they are made.
4. **Before, with a loop after.** Decide before money moves. Report what happened afterwards.
5. **Signed on both ends, bound to the payment.** The query is signed by the requester, the decision by the authority, the receipt countersigned by the executor, the outcome by its reporter. The decision names the payment and the executor it is for; presented anywhere else it is void.
6. **Portable.** A receipt verifies offline against published keys.
7. **Thresholds, not histories.** The authority discloses signed predicates about an actor, never another executor's data.
8. **Fail closed, with a path.** Every non-allowed decision names what would change it.
9. **Milliseconds, with a stated budget.** The executor publishes its budget. A timeout is denied, or escalated to the executor's own desk when its policy says so, never allowed; the executor signs that itself as a local decision (section 4.6), never as the authority's.
10. **Status-code independent.** Headers carry the protocol. The status codes in section 6 are the canonical mapping, not a requirement.

## 3. Roles

- **Actor chain.** Human principal, then operator (the business that runs the agent), then agent (a registered identity with a key), then instance (a session with a short-lived token). Each hop is `verified` with a stated basis, `asserted`, or `absent`.
- **Requester.** Whoever sends the query: the operator's wallet, card program or payment runtime on the buyer leg; the executor on the merchant leg.
- **Executor.** The party that moves the money.
- **Risk authority.** Computes and signs the decision. kredit is the reference. The protocol allows any authority, and an executor may chain several.
- **Reporters.** Identity vendors, fraud engines, registries, networks and facilitators whose artifacts arrive as evidence or whose outcomes close the loop.
- **Verifier.** A dispute desk, an auditor, a regulator, or the next executor, checking a receipt offline.

## 4. Objects

All objects are JSON. Signed objects are canonicalized with RFC 8785 (JCS) and signed as compact JWS. `ES256` is required of every implementation, `EdDSA` is optional, and a parallel slot for a post-quantum signature is reserved. A decision may also be wrapped as a W3C Verifiable Credential (`jwt_vc_json`).

Hashes x403 makes are `sha256:` followed by lowercase hex of the canonical form. A hash or id made by another protocol (an AP2 `checkout_hash`, a Verifiable Intent `transaction_id`, which are base64url and not sha256) is carried verbatim in a field named for it, and x403 records its own `sha256:` hash of that artifact beside it as a `_ref` field. Amounts are decimal strings. Times are RFC 3339. Keys: executors publish a JWK set at `/.well-known/x403/jwks.json`; authorities publish `/.well-known/x403/authority.json` or resolve a `did:web`.

JSON Schemas for every object are in `schemas/`. Test vectors with fixed keys, fixed times and negative cases are in `test-vectors/`; an implementation passes when it gives every result `expectations.json` names.

### 4.1 Payment

The thing about to happen.

```json
{
  "type": "payment",
  "amount": "189.00", "currency": "USD",
  "rail": "card",
  "executor": { "id": "bestbuy.com", "category": "5732", "jurisdiction": "US" },
  "counterparty": { "id": "bestbuy.com", "kind": "merchant", "age_days": 9124 },
  "items": [{ "sku": "6421743", "description": "USB-C hub", "class": "electronics", "qty": 1 }],
  "references": {
    "order_id": "BBY-2210-7731",
    "cart_ref": "sha256:…",
    "ap2_checkout_hash": "<as AP2 made it>",
    "payment_requirements_ref": "sha256:…"
  },
  "idempotency_key": "ord_BBY-2210-7731"
}
```

`payment_requirements_ref` binds the decision to the exact x402 `PaymentRequirements` or MPP challenge, by x403's hash of it. `cart_ref` is x403's hash of the cart artifact; `ap2_checkout_hash` and `vi_transaction_id` carry the AP2 `checkout_hash` and the Verifiable Intent `transaction_id` verbatim, in their own formats. `instruction_id` binds it to a Visa VIC instruction. The earlier names `cart_hash` and `payment_requirements_hash` are still read. A decision is for one payment: change the payment and you need a new decision. The hash of this object, as presented, is the `subject.payment_ref` of the decision.

### 4.2 Actor presentation

The chain, hop by hop.

```json
{
  "human":    { "status": "verified", "basis": "x401", "assurance": "IAL2", "ref": "sha256:…" },
  "operator": { "status": "verified", "basis": "kyb", "id": "did:web:acme.com", "via": "kredit" },
  "agent":    { "status": "verified", "basis": "registry", "id": "did:key:z6Mk…",
                "registry": [{ "name": "visa-tap" }, { "name": "erc-8004", "chain": "arc" }, { "name": "kredit-passport" }],
                "harness": "claude-code", "model": "haiku4.5", "attestation": null },
  "instance": { "status": "verified", "basis": "token", "token_ref": "sha256:…",
                "issued_at": "…", "expires_at": "…", "origin": "https://agent.acme.com" },
  "session":  { "ip": "…", "device": "…", "ua": "…", "provenance": "mcp" }
}
```

An unregistered agent still gets a decision, usually `escalated` on first contact and then a file. Lacking a registration is a reason code, not a refusal.

### 4.3 Intent

What the agent says it is doing, and the mandate that lets it.

```json
{
  "mandate": {
    "format": "ap2",
    "ref": "sha256:…",
    "scope": { "executors": ["bestbuy.com"], "categories": ["electronics"],
               "max_amount": "250.00", "window": "2026-10-01/2026-10-31", "budget": "3000.00/month" },
    "issued_by": "human", "signed": true
  },
  "declared": { "goal": "replace broken USB-C hub for Denver office", "expected_amount_range": ["50", "200"] },
  "task_ref": "sha256:…"
}
```

A mismatch between `declared`, the payment, and `mandate.scope` becomes a reason code. It is not an automatic denial: the executor's policy decides what a mismatch is worth.

### 4.4 Risk query

```json
{
  "scheme": "x403", "version": "0.1",
  "policy_id": "bestbuy-checkout-v3",
  "direction": "inbound",
  "mode": "inline",
  "payment": { }, "actor": { }, "intent": { },
  "evidence": [
    { "type": "x401.result", "issuer": "did:web:x401-issuer.example", "ref": "sha256:…", "inline": "<jwt>" },
    { "type": "ap2.payment_mandate", "ref": "sha256:…", "inline": "<sd-jwt>" },
    { "type": "fraud.vendor", "issuer": "vendor.example", "ref": "sha256:…", "inline": "<jws>" },
    { "type": "x403.receipt", "issuer": "did:web:othermerchant.com", "ref": "sha256:…" }
  ],
  "challenge": { "nonce": "…", "expires_at": "…" },
  "budget_ms": 150,
  "on_timeout": "denied",
  "requester": { "id": "did:web:bestbuy.com", "kid": "2026-10" },
  "requester_sig": "<JWS>"
}
```

`direction` is `inbound` when the executor asks about a payment arriving, `outbound` when a wallet asks about a payment leaving. `mode` is `inline` (decide now, act on it), `preflight` (decide now, pay later citing the id) or `shadow` (decide, log, do not act). `on_timeout` is `denied` or `escalated` and nothing else: an allow on timeout would make a slow authority a bypass. `budget_ms` is the authority's time from receipt of the query to the first byte of its answer, at least 20 and at most 10000.

### 4.5 Decision

```json
{
  "scheme": "x403", "version": "0.1",
  "id": "k_950a4d",
  "decision": "allowed",
  "direction": "inbound", "mode": "inline",
  "subject": { "payment_ref": "sha256:…", "amount": "189.00", "currency": "USD", "executor_id": "bestbuy.com" },
  "audience": "bestbuy.com",
  "query_ref": "sha256:…",
  "policy_id": "bestbuy-checkout-v3", "policy_hash": "sha256:…",
  "authority": { "id": "did:web:api.kredit.sh", "kid": "…" },
  "verified": { "human": "x401:IAL2", "operator": "kyb", "agent": "visa-tap+erc-8004", "instance": "token", "session": "present" },
  "checks": [
    { "key": "identity", "status": "pass" },
    { "key": "mandate",  "status": "pass" },
    { "key": "payee",    "status": "pass" },
    { "key": "behavior", "status": "warn", "reasons": ["first_use_of_product_class"] }
  ],
  "reasons": ["first_use_of_product_class", "amount_at_cap"],
  "constraints": { "max_amount": "250.00", "currency": "USD", "human_stepup_above": "100.00", "expires_at": "…" },
  "evidence_hashes": ["sha256:<query>", "sha256:<x401 result>", "sha256:<mandate>"],
  "disclosure": { "history": "threshold", "claims": [{ "clean_payments_gte": 50 }, { "disputes_12m_lte": 0 }] },
  "binding_hints": { "http_status": 200 },
  "supersedes": null,
  "issued_at": "…", "expires_at": "…",
  "decision_sig": "<JWS by authority>"
}
```

- `subject` is the one payment this decision is for: the hash of the payment object as presented, its amount, currency and executor. `audience` is the executor it is for. Both are inside the signature. A verifier MUST check both before trusting an allow: a decision presented at an executor other than its audience, or for a payment other than its subject, is void, with reason `audience_mismatch` or `subject_mismatch`. `query_ref` is the hash of the query as received.
- `checks` is the executor-facing summary, four keys, worst-of. Whatever the authority computes behind them stays behind them.
- `reasons` are keys of the registry (section 8), in the order they matter.
- `constraints` is a property of an allowed decision: the yes is scoped by a ceiling, a step-up line, the rails and executors it covers, and an expiry.
- `escalation` is present only on an escalated decision, and its `kind` names who resolves it: `agent_adjust` (shrink the amount or scope and retry, no human needed), `operator_confirm`, `human_stepup`, `executor_review`. `options` says how, `reduce_to` says to what, `retry_after` says when.
- `binding_hints.http_status` is the status the executor should answer with if it uses the HTTP binding, so the mapping in section 6 lives in the object and not in every client. It is advisory and outside the decision's meaning; an executor may override it. Earlier decisions carried it as `http_status` at the top level, which verifiers still read.
- `supersedes` links a re-decision to the hash of the one it replaces. `/.well-known/x403/revoked.json` lists decisions by hash for the same reason: an id can be reissued, a hash cannot.
- There is no `latency_ms` and no numeric risk, trust or confidence value of any kind. Timing stays in the authority's logs.
- Decisions are single use. An executor that executes the same decision twice has a bug, and the authority will say `decision_consumed` the second time. An idempotent re-query (same `idempotency_key`, same payment) returns the same decision and does not consume it.

### 4.6 Local decision

What the executor signs itself when the authority gave no decision inside `budget_ms`, or could not be reached. It is never an allow, and it is never presented as the authority's: `local` is true, `authority` names the authority that did not answer, and the signature is the executor's.

```json
{
  "scheme": "x403", "version": "0.2",
  "id": "local_1a2b",
  "decision": "denied",
  "local": true,
  "direction": "inbound",
  "subject": { "payment_ref": "sha256:…", "amount": "189.00", "currency": "USD", "executor_id": "bestbuy.com" },
  "audience": "bestbuy.com",
  "policy_id": "bestbuy-checkout-v3",
  "authority": { "id": "did:web:api.kredit.sh" },
  "reasons": ["authority_timeout"],
  "binding_hints": { "http_status": 403 },
  "executor": { "id": "bestbuy.com", "kid": "…" },
  "issued_at": "…", "expires_at": "…",
  "local_sig": "<JWS by executor>"
}
```

`decision` is `denied`, or `escalated` with `kind: executor_review` when the query's `on_timeout` said so. The executor SHOULD sign it; a verifier checks it against the executor's keys, never an authority's. The executor files it with the authority as a receipt (section 4.7, `local_decision`), and the authority stores it as such and never lists it among its own decisions. Schema: `schemas/local_decision.json`.

### 4.7 Receipt

```json
{
  "decision_ref": "k_950a4d", "decision_hash": "sha256:…",
  "executed": true, "executed_at": "…",
  "execution": { "rail": "card", "ref": "auth_7c…", "amount": "189.00", "currency": "USD" },
  "executor": { "id": "did:web:bestbuy.com", "kid": "…" },
  "executor_sig": "<JWS>"
}
```

A receipt is the decision plus the executor's countersignature. It is the dispute-file artifact, and what fills an a-comm AEP `policy` artifact (`outcome`, `rules_triggered`, `policy_engine_vendor`, `policy_version`). A receipt for a local decision carries the signed local decision as `local_decision` and its id as `decision_ref`; the authority files it without a decision of its own behind it.

### 4.8 Outcome report

```json
{
  "decision_ref": "k_950a4d", "decision_hash": "sha256:…",
  "authority_id": "did:web:api.kredit.sh",
  "outcome": "settled", "category": "none", "reported_via": "executor",
  "at": "…", "amount": "189.00", "currency": "USD", "reason_code": null,
  "reporter": { "id": "did:web:bestbuy.com", "kid": "…" }, "sig": "<JWS>"
}
```

Outcomes: `settled`, `delivered`, `disputed`, `chargeback`, `refund`, `reversal`, `fraud_confirmed`, `false_positive`. The last two are the ones that make the next decision better. `category` says what kind of loss, if any: `attack`, `accident`, `control_failure`, `commercial_dispute`, `none`. `reported_via` says which channel the news came through. More than one outcome may be reported for a decision; they are read in `at` order. `decision_hash` ties the report to the decision as signed, so it cannot be moved to another decision with the same id.

Who may report: the executor named in the receipt, the requester named in the query, the authority itself, and reporters registered in the executor's policy document. The report is signed by its reporter; the authority MUST verify the signature against the reporter's published keys and MUST reject a report from anyone else.

## 5. Flows

**F1. Inbound, inline (merchant leg).** The agent attempts a payment. The executor builds a query from the request plus whatever x401, TAP, SPT or mandate artifacts arrived, signs it, and posts it to its authority with a budget. The authority verifies the evidence signatures, resolves the actor chain, evaluates against the executor's policy and returns a signed decision bound to this payment and this executor. If no decision arrives inside the budget, the executor signs a local decision instead. The executor maps it to a status. The agent: allowed, done, within `constraints`; escalated with `agent_adjust`, shrink and retry citing the id; escalated with another kind, push it to the operator or human and retry with `step_up_ref`; denied, stop and carry the signed decision back to the operator. The executor posts a receipt, and later an outcome.

**F2. Outbound, before the payment leaves (buyer leg).** The operator's wallet, card program's real-time authorization webhook or vault proxy builds a query from the agent's own intent and mandate, calls its authority, and releases the payment only when allowed. The decision id and `subject` ride with the payment. The merchant verifies the signature and the subject offline. No merchant change is required.

**F3. Preflight before a final-settlement rail.** `HEAD` with a presentation, then allowed with `expires_at` and `constraints`, then pay over x402 or MPP citing the decision id, then the merchant checks the id against its own record or the authority's signature, then settle. This is what makes final-settlement rails safe: decide before paying.

**F4. Shadow.** `mode: "shadow"`. The authority returns a decision, the executor logs it and does not act, outcomes still flow back. This is the pilot shape.

**F5. Escalate and re-evaluate.** Modelled on OpenID AuthZEN AARP. The non-allowed decision names a prerequisite; the requester satisfies it; a new decision is issued with `supersedes` pointing at the old one; the old one is consumed.

## 6. HTTP binding

Two bindings carry the same objects. Header values are base64url JSON without padding. Every response that carries an x403 header MUST also carry `Cache-Control: no-store`: signed objects are never cached by an intermediary. A single header value MUST NOT exceed 8 KB; headers carry objects and references, and anything with inline evidence travels in a body with `Content-Type: application/x403+json`. An executor treats an oversized or unreadable presentation header as no presentation.

### 6.1 Header binding

The launch binding. It matches the x401 and x402 convention.

| Header | Direction | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `X403-Required` | executor to agent | a risk query the agent must help satisfy, or a non-allowed decision |
| `X403-Presentation` | agent to executor | actor presentation, intent and evidence, or the id of a prior preflight decision |
| `X403-Decision` | executor to agent | the signed decision |

### 6.2 Auth-scheme binding

The IETF path, modelled on MPP's `WWW-Authenticate: Payment`.

```
WWW-Authenticate: Risk policy="bestbuy-checkout-v3", realm="checkout", request="<base64url query>", expires="…"
Authorization: Risk <base64url presentation>
Risk-Decision: <base64url decision>
```

### 6.3 Status mapping

The decision carries `binding_hints.http_status`. This is how it is computed. An executor may override it, and must not invent a status outside this table for an x403 answer. A 403 without an `X403-Decision` or `Risk-Decision` header is not an x403 answer: clients MUST read the decision from the header, never from the status.

| Decision | Status | Meaning on the wire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| allowed, with or without constraints | 200, 201 or 202 | the resource, plus `X403-Decision` |
| escalated, `agent_adjust` | 403 | the decision says what to shrink to; the agent retries citing the id |
| escalated, `operator_confirm` or `human_stepup` | 428 | a precondition is required: present it, then retry with `step_up_ref` |
| escalated, `executor_review` | 403 | a person on the executor's side decides; `retry_after` says when to ask again |
| denied, identity group | 401 | the chain failed: unverified human, expired instance token, revoked credential |
| denied, compliance group | 451 | sanctions, jurisdiction, or a legal threshold |
| denied, velocity group | 429 | too much, too fast; `retry_after` is set |
| denied, any other group | 403 | understood, and refused on policy |
| replay or expiry: `nonce_consumed`, `decision_expired`, `decision_consumed`, `decision_superseded` | 409 | the state of the conversation conflicts with the request |
| `policy_unknown`, `presentation_malformed`, `evidence_hash_mismatch` | 400 | the request itself is wrong |
| `authority_timeout` | 403 | the executor's own local decision (section 4.6) in the header, signed by the executor, denied or escalated per `on_timeout` |
| `audience_mismatch`, `subject_mismatch`, `presentation_signature_invalid` | 400 | a decision or presentation for another executor or payment |
| non-disclosure denied | 404 | no header at all, by the executor's choice |

Six codes are normative for clients: 200, 401, 403, 428, 429, 451. A client library must handle all six. The rest are executor choices a client may treat as 403.

### 6.4 Preflight

`HEAD` or `OPTIONS` with `X403-Presentation` answers `200` with `X403-Decision` and no body. The decision id is then cited in the x402 `PaymentPayload.extensions`, the MPP `Authorization: Payment` payload, or the AP2 Payment Mandate `risk_data`.

### 6.5 Discovery

- `/.well-known/x403/policy/<id>.json`: required hops and evidence, step-up lines, accepted authorities and mandate formats, latency budget, timeout behaviour. No weights.
- `/.well-known/x403/revoked.json`: revoked decisions by hash (the id beside it for readers), and retired policy hashes. Served with `Cache-Control` of at most 60 seconds.
- `/.well-known/x403/authority.json`: an authority's id, keys, endpoints, algorithms and the evidence types it reads.
- `/.well-known/x403/jwks.json`: the keys.

### 6.6 The ladder

`403, 401, 402, 200`: risk posture first, then identity at the level the posture requires, then payment, then execution with the decision attached. Or `401, 403, 402, 200` where identity is needed to evaluate at all. The policy document says which.

## 7. Other bindings

- **Authority API.** `POST /x403/decide`, `POST /x403/receipts`, `POST /x403/outcomes`, `GET /x403/decisions/{id}`, `GET /x403/reason-codes`, and the well-known documents.
- **MCP.** One tool, `x403`, with an `action`: `decide`, `preflight`, `get`, `receipt`, `outcome`, `revoke`, `authority`, `jwks`, `policy`, `codes`, `revoked`. An agent's payment runtime asks before it pays.
- **A2A.** The decision as a task message part before settlement.
- **Verifiable Credential.** The decision as a VC, so it rides inside an AP2 Payment Mandate (`risk_data`), an x401 result, a UCP `signals` entry or an AEP policy artifact.
- **Card authorization.** `id` and `policy_hash` as merchant-defined data in the authorization message. The networks' agentic transaction indicator is the slot it eventually occupies.
- **Stablecoin.** `id` in x402 `extensions`; preflight before settlement; the receiving side verifies offline.

## 8. Reason codes

The registry is `registry/reason-codes.json`, served by every authority at `/x403/reason-codes`. Each code belongs to one **group**, each group folds into one of the four **checks** and names the status a denied decision on it produces, and each code carries a default **effect**: `denied`, `escalated` (with the kind), or `note`. The effect is the default; the executor's policy may be stricter or looser, and the decision says what the policy did.

| Group | Check | Status | Codes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| identity | identity | 401 | `human_unverified`, `human_assurance_below_policy`, `operator_unverified`, `operator_pending`, `agent_unregistered`, `agent_unverified`, `agent_pending`, `agent_frozen`, `agent_key_rotated_recently`, `instance_token_missing`, `instance_token_expired`, `instance_token_revoked`, `credential_vouched`, `credential_absent`, `origin_changed`, `relay_mismatch` |
| mandate | mandate | 403 | `mandate_absent`, `mandate_unsigned`, `mandate_expired`, `mandate_revoked`, `off_mandate_category`, `off_mandate_executor`, `over_mandate_amount`, `over_mandate_budget`, `intent_payment_mismatch`, `intent_counterparty_mismatch`, `po_mismatch` |
| scope | mandate | 403 | `delegation_depth_exceeded`, `scope_not_inherited`, `separation_violation` |

## 9. Evidence

An evidence item is `{ type, issuer, ref, inline?, jwks_url? }`. `ref` is the hash of the artifact. `inline` carries the artifact when the authority is expected to verify it. The authority records each item as `verified` (signature checked against the issuer's keys, hash matches, not expired), `asserted` (hash matches, signature not checkable by this authority), or `invalid` (hash mismatch, bad signature, expired), and the result lands on the decision as `verified` per hop and as reason codes.

Types this version names: `x401.result`, `x403.receipt`, `x403.decision`, `ap2.intent_mandate`, `ap2.cart_mandate`, `ap2.payment_mandate`, `verifiable-intent.chain`, `amex.proof_of_intent`, `visa.vic_instruction`, `visa.tap_signature`, `mastercard.wba_signature`, `skyfire.kya`, `skyfire.pay`, `kite.passport`, `stripe.shared_payment_token`, `mpp.challenge`, `x402.payment_requirements`, `erc-8004.registration`, `kredit.passport`, `kredit.agent_token`, `circle.screening`, `vgs.alias`, `fraud.vendor`, `other`.

An authority publishes which of these it verifies in `authority.json` under `evidence_read`. Anything else it records as `asserted` with `evidence_unsupported` on the decision.

## 10. Trust model

- Executor and authority exchange signed objects over TLS. Keys are published both ways.
- Decisions verify offline against the authority's published key, and are then checked for `audience`, `subject`, expiry, and absence from the revocation list. Receipts verify against the executor's. A local decision verifies against the executor's key and is never an allow.
- The actor chain is verified hop by hop and recorded as `verified`, `asserted` or `absent` with its basis. A hop without proof is a reason code, not a failure.
- Policy belongs to the executor; computation belongs to the authority. Only a verified human may change a policy version, and the policy hash on every decision says which version decided it.
- Replay and relay are stopped by `challenge.nonce`, `idempotency_key`, `expires_at`, single-use decisions, `audience` and `subject`.
- Outcomes are accepted only from the executor named in the receipt, the requester, the authority and the reporters registered in the policy, each verified by signature.
- Revocation is published at `/.well-known/x403/revoked.json`. Instance tokens are revoked at the authority.
- Keys: executor `jwks.json`; authority document or DID; agent keys per the registry they come from.

## 11. Privacy

- No raw cross-executor history leaves the authority. `disclosure.claims` are signed threshold predicates. A later version adds zero-knowledge range proofs.
- Executors see reasons, never other executors' data.
- Presentations are selective: only the hops and claims the published policy asks for.
- Retention: receipts for the rail's dispute window; outcomes pseudonymized at the authority.
- Lawful basis: fraud prevention for the executor; the authority is a processor for the query and a controller for its own record.

## 12. Security considerations

Each threat, and the field or rule that answers it.

| Threat | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Replay of a decision | single use (`decision_consumed`), `expires_at`, `nonce_consumed` |
| Relay of a decision to another executor | `audience`, checked by every verifier |
| Laundering a decision onto another payment | `subject` (`payment_ref`, amount, currency, executor), checked by every verifier |
| Moving an outcome to another decision with the same id | `decision_hash` on the outcome |
| Outcome poisoning | only the receipt's executor, the requester, the authority and registered reporters may report; signatures verified |
| Impersonating the authority | keys from the well-known documents, pinned authority id, `authority.kid` in every decision |
| Forcing a timeout to get a yes | `on_timeout` is never an allow; a local decision is executor-signed, never the authority's |
| Rolling a policy back | `policy_hash` on every decision; retired hashes on the revocation list |
| Key compromise | retired keys stay published for verification; revocation by hash |
| A tampered object | the signature is over the canonical form of the whole object; a changed field is a different payload |
| Header overflow and cache leakage | 8 KB per header value, bodies as `application/x403+json`, `Cache-Control: no-store` on every response that carries an x403 header |
| Reading the status instead of the decision | a 403 without an `X403-Decision` header is not an x403 answer |

## 13. What a receipt proves

That a named authority, under a named policy version, given the hashed evidence, issued this decision for this payment to this executor at this time, and that a named executor acted on it. Not that upstream facts were true, that settlement happened, or who is liable. Liability is the rails' and the regulators' question. x403 gives them the record they have said they lack.

## 14. Conformance

An **authority** conforms when it: signs decisions with ES256 over the RFC 8785 form; sets `subject`, `audience` and `query_ref`; publishes `authority.json`, `jwks.json`, `reason-codes`; honours `budget_ms` and `on_timeout`; refuses a consumed nonce and a consumed decision; accepts receipts, local decisions as receipts, and outcomes only from authorized, signed reporters; and never places a numeric risk value in a decision.

An **executor** conforms when it: asks before executing; checks `audience` and `subject` on any decision it did not receive directly from its authority; maps decisions to status per section 6.3 or documents its own mapping; attaches `X403-Decision` and `Cache-Control: no-store` to the response; publishes its policy document and keys; produces a signed local decision on timeout and files it; posts a receipt for every execution and an outcome when it knows one.

A **client** conforms when it: reads the decision from the header or body, not the status; handles the six normative codes; retries an `agent_adjust` within `reduce_to`; carries a non-allowed decision back to its operator unchanged.

An **offline verifier** checks, in this order: the schema; `decision_sig` against the authority's published keys; `audience` against the executor presenting it; `subject` against the payment; `expires_at` against `executed_at`; absence from the revocation list; `policy_hash` not retired; the receipt's `executor_sig`; the receipt's `decision_hash` against the decision as signed.
